Choose a cause

Millions of Canadians live with dry mouth (xerostomia). It is most often a side effect of medication, cancer treatment or a health condition, and it can make speaking, eating and sleeping hard.

All causes {{icon:arrow-right}}
{{cause:medications}}

Medication

Many medications can cause dry mouth as a side effect. Check out our list or ask your healthcare professional if your medication may be one of them.

Medication list {{icon:arrow-right}}
{{cause:cpap}}

C-PAP and sleep apnea

Sleep apnea often leads to mouth breathing, and the constant flow of air from using a CPAP machine can dry out the oral cavity leading to oral health issues.

CPAP and dry mouth {{icon:arrow-right}}
{{cause:sjogren}}

Sjögren’s disease

While the effects of Sjögren’s disease can be widespread, inflammation of the salivary glands is common and results in dry mouth, being one of the main symptoms.

Sjögren’s and dry mouth {{icon:arrow-right}}
{{cause:cancer}}

Cancer treatment

Many cancer treatments, including radiation for head and neck cancer, can directly affect the salivary glands, resulting in decreased saliva production and dry mouth.

Cancer and dry mouth {{icon:arrow-right}}
{{cause:cannabis}}

Medical cannabis

Cannabis use decreases saliva production, causing dry mouth and increasing the risk of tooth decay and gum disease.

Cannabis and dry mouth {{icon:arrow-right}}
{{cause:diabetes}}

Diabetes

Diabetes can cause dry mouth due to decreased salivary function caused by high blood sugar in saliva, frequent urination and dehydration, as well as from diabetic medications.

Diabetes and dry mouth {{icon:arrow-right}}
{{cause:neuro}}

Neurological diseases

Alzheimer’s, Parkinson’s disease, ALS and others can cause dry mouth due to changes in salivary production. Dry mouth is also a side effect of many of the medications used to treat these diseases.

Neurological diseases {{icon:arrow-right}}
{{cause:dysphagia}}

Dysphagia

Dry mouth can make difficulty swallowing worse. Food cannot move through the mouth and esophagus easily without enough saliva. Medications can increase the incidence of dry mouth.

Dysphagia and dry mouth {{icon:arrow-right}}
{{cause:other}}

Other causes

Many medical conditions including HIV/AIDS, mast cell activation syndrome and many rare diseases often cause dry mouth. Stroke patients, nursing home residents and others in chronic caregiver settings are also at risk.

Other causes {{icon:arrow-right}}

Exclusive technology

Born at the Institut Pasteur

In the 1950s, Professor Baranger, a member of the research team at the Institut Pasteur in France, discovered that the natural oxygenation process of certain vegetable oils brought about a fundamental change in the molecular structure of oils, transforming them into a lipid-based substance with therapeutic properties.

OGT

Aquoral contains Oxidized Glycerol Triester (OGT) produced through a process of controlled oxidation of corn oil, a patented technology exclusive to Aquoral.

Film

The OGT in Aquoral bonds to the mucous membranes, forming a protective film over the whole of the mucous membranes of the mouth. This lipid film lubricates the oral cavity and prevents the loss of moisture from the tissues, thus reducing the tendency for dryness of the mucous membrane in people with reduced salivary function.

Important safety information

Ingredients: Oxidized glycerol triesters (TGO), silicon dioxide, aspartame, and artificial flavouring.

Actions: Aquoral® is a lipid-based solution resembling human saliva designed to moisten and lubricate the oral cavity, including the oral mucosa of the mouth, tongue and throat, by formation of a lipid film which limits loss of water and restores the viscoelasticity of the oral mucosa. Aquoral also provides protective action against further inflammation of the oral mucosa. Xerostomia (dry mouth) has harmful effects on the oral cavity and quality of life: consequently, management of dry mouth is primarily based on relief of symptoms.

Indications: Aquoral is intended to provide relief from chronic and temporary xerostomia (dry mouth), which may be a result of disease such as Sjögren’s, oral inflammation, medication, chemo or radiotherapy, stress or aging. Aquoral relieves symptoms of dry mouth such as difficulties in swallowing, speech, and changes in taste.

Contraindications: Aquoral is contraindicated for anyone with a known history of hypersensitivity to any of its ingredients. Contains aspartame (a source of phenylalanine); not for people with phenylketonuria (PKU).

Precautions: Read package insert carefully before using this spray. Avoid contact with eyes. Flush eyes with water if accidental introduction into eyes should occur.

Interactions: There are no known (TGO) interactions with medicinal or other products.

Directions for use: Shake gently. One dose (2 sprays) into the mouth 3 to 4 times a day. Spread product on to inflamed and/or dry areas of the mouth with the tongue. Pump may require priming for initial use.

Net contents: 2 canisters, 10 mL (0.338 fl oz) each.
