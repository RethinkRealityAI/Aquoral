Who it helps

Cancer

Dry mouth is a common side effect of radiation or chemotherapy. Here is why it matters and how to protect your mouth.

Dry Mouth is a common side effect of radiation or chemotherapy for cancer patients. Often times, chemo and dry mouth go hand in hand. Approximately 90-100% of head and neck cancer patients treated with radiation will develop oral complications, with Dry Mouth being the most common. It is important to take steps before and during treatment to protect against the discomfort and problems associated with Dry Mouth.

Dry Mouth can develop into mucositis if left untreated. It is a debilitating complication of cancer treatments and can lead to several problems, including pain, the inability to eat, and an increased risk of infection due to open mouth sores.

Patients have often used “saliva substitutes” as part of the prevention and treatment of cancer-related dry mouth. Aquoral can be an important addition to your Dry Mouth strategy, helping to prevent mucositis and positively impacting a patient’s quality of life while going through cancer treatment.

  • Unlike most over-the-counter water-based products, Aquoral’s lipid-based formulation lubricates and creates a protective barrier helping trap moisture in the oral mucosa and helping prevent cancer-related oral problems such as mucositis
  • Aquoral can be used alone as a single agent or in combination as a cost-effective way to “lock in” the moisturizing effects and prolong the benefit of other dry mouth treatments
  • Aquoral is available in a small lightweight spray that can be easily carried during daily activities

Protecting your mouth during treatment

Here are a few additional steps you can take to help moisturize and protect your mouth during treatment:

  1. Stay hydrated: It’s important for cancer patients to drink plenty of water and other fluids to keep the mouth moist. Sipping water throughout the day is helpful.
  2. Avoid tobacco and alcohol: Both tobacco and alcohol can dry out the mouth and exacerbate the symptoms of Dry Mouth. It’s best to avoid them altogether.
  3. Choose moist foods: Eating moist foods such as soups, stews, and fruits can help to keep the mouth moist.
  4. Practice good oral hygiene: Brushing and flossing regularly can help prevent dental problems that can make Dry Mouth worse.
  5. Talk to your healthcare provider: If you are experiencing Dry Mouth, talk to your healthcare provider or oncology team about how Aquoral can fit into your mouth-care routine.
