CPAP and sleep apnea

Sleep apnea and the CPAP machines that treat it can both leave your mouth dry overnight. Aquoral can help.

Sleep apnea can cause dry mouth because the condition often leads to mouth breathing. This causes air to pass over the tongue and soft palate, leading to a dry mouth and throat.

What is even more challenging is that CPAP machines, which are designed to combat sleep apnea, can also cause Dry Mouth because the constant flow of air can dry out the oral cavity. The pressurized air from the machine often forces the mouth to open, which further dries out the mouth and can lead to oral health issues.

Users are often faced with the dilemma of trying to fix one problem while creating another. Aquoral can help deliver a restful night’s sleep by mitigating the side effects of dry mouth while also preventing long-term damage. The lipid-based formulation creates a protective oral shield and lubricates the mucosa, providing relief for 4 to 6 hours.

Relief for dry mouth from sleep apnea

Aquoral is a dry mouth spray designed to provide relief for individuals suffering from dry mouth due to sleep apnea and the use of CPAP machines. It works by forming a protective, lubricating film over the mucous membranes in the mouth, which helps to retain moisture and reduce the sensation of dryness. This can significantly improve comfort during sleep, aiding those who rely on CPAP therapy to manage their sleep apnea, and ultimately enhancing their overall sleep quality and oral health.

Dry mouth caused by CPAP machines

CPAP machines, which are widely used to treat sleep apnea, can often lead to dry mouth as a side effect. This condition occurs primarily because the continuous positive airway pressure that keeps the airway open may also increase air flow through the mouth, especially in individuals who breathe through their mouths during sleep. The constant flow of air can dry out the mucous membranes, leading to discomfort and increased risk of oral health issues such as gum disease and tooth decay. The use of a CPAP mask that doesn’t fit properly can exacerbate mouth dryness by allowing air to escape.
