Neurological diseases

Parkinson’s, multiple sclerosis, ALS and stroke can all affect saliva production and lead to dry mouth.

Neurological diseases can significantly impact various bodily functions, including salivary production, leading to dry mouth also known as xerostomia. Conditions such as Parkinson’s disease are particularly notorious for causing this symptom due to the degeneration of neurons that control salivation. Multiple sclerosis can also lead to dry mouth as the disease can disrupt the signals between the brain and salivary glands. Amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS) may affect muscle control, including those necessary for the normal function of salivary glands, resulting in reduced saliva flow. Additionally, patients with stroke-related complications may experience dry mouth due to the impairment of nerve pathways involved in saliva production.
