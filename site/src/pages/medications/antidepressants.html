Dry Mouth from Antidepressants

Dry mouth, or xerostomia, is a common yet often overlooked side effect of many medications, including antidepressants. It is estimated that up to 46 percent of patients taking antidepressants experience dry mouth as a side effect. This can be uncomfortable and have negative impacts on oral health, so it's important to understand why this happens and how to manage it.

Popular Types of Antidepressants

Antidepressants are utilized for managing depression and anxiety, with Wellbutrin, Lexapro, and Zoloft being among the most commonly used. While effective for mental health treatment, these medications can disrupt normal saliva production leading to dry mouth.

Other medications that can cause dry mouth include certain antihistamines, muscle relaxants, and blood pressure medications.

Why Antidepressants Can Cause Dry Mouth

Antidepressants can affect the body's muscarinic receptors which play a role in saliva secretion. As a result, patients may experience dry mouth symptoms including difficulty swallowing, sore throat, and a significant impact on their oral health.

This side effect is also seen with other medications like trazodone, contributing to the overall concentration of trazodone dry mouth cases.

Understanding the Long-Term Effects of Dry Mouth

Unchecked, dry mouth can lead to severe dental health issues, including tooth decay, gum disease, and mouth infections.

It's crucial for patients experiencing dry mouth from antidepressants to seek treatment to mitigate these risks and preserve their oral health over the long term.
