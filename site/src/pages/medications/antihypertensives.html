How blood-pressure medicines (antihypertensives) can cause dry mouth, and how Aquoral helps.

Dry Mouth from Antihypertensives

Dry mouth, also known as xerostomia, frequently emerges as a side effect of various medications, notably antihypertensives. It's estimated that a significant portion of individuals on blood pressure-lowering drugs might experience this condition. The sensation of a perpetually dry mouth not only causes discomfort but can also adversely affect one's dental health. Persistent dry mouth may also affect strict adherence to the medications as patients seek some relief by reducing the amount of medication they take. Recognizing the underlying reasons for this occurrence and finding effective ways to mitigate it are crucial for those affected.

Popular Types of Antihypertensive

Antihypertensive medications, including ACE inhibitors like lisinopril and ARBs such as losartan, are commonly used to manage high blood pressure. These medications can lead to dry mouth as a side effect. Other common antihypertensives like beta-blockers metoprolol, amlodipine, and hydrochlorothiazide may also cause dry mouth.

Why Antihypertensive Can Cause Dry Mouth

Antihypertensives may alter the function of muscarinic receptors, which are integral to the process of saliva secretion. Consequently, patients might notice symptoms linked to dry mouth, including difficulties in swallowing, a constant sore throat, and adverse effects on their oral health. This reaction is comparable to the dry mouth effects observed with medications like antidepressants, contributing broadly to the phenomenon of medication-induced xerostomia.

Understanding the Long-Term Effects of Dry Mouth

Unchecked, dry mouth can lead to severe dental health issues, including tooth decay, gum disease, and mouth infections.

It's crucial for patients experiencing dry mouth from antihypertensives to seek treatment to mitigate these risks and preserve their oral health over the long term.
