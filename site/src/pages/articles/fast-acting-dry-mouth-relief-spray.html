Fast-acting dry mouth relief spray: why Aquoral Spray works better

How a lipid-based spray delivers rapid, longer-lasting relief from xerostomia.

Xerostomia (dry mouth) is a common condition that can harm oral health and overall wellbeing. It results from insufficient salivary gland secretion and can cause discomfort and secondary oral complications. This article reviews common causes of xerostomia, the oral-health consequences, the mechanism by which Aquoral Spray delivers rapid relief, correct application technique, and the clinical data supporting its use.

Epidemiological research consistently documents the prevalence of xerostomia and its substantial impact on daily function and quality of life.

Xerostomia’s impact on oral health and quality of life

Xerostomia produces marked and consistent detriments to patients’ day to day functioning. Reported prevalence estimates in representative samples of older adults range from 12% to 47%, with approximately 10% reported in individuals in their early thirties.

The impact of xerostomia on oral-health-related quality of life among younger adults, WM Thomson, 2006

What causes dry mouth and how does it affect oral health?

Xerostomia may arise from multiple causes, including systemic disease, medications, and behavioural factors. Identifying the underlying cause is required for targeted intervention.

Which medical conditions and treatments lead to xerostomia?

Several medical conditions and treatments are associated with reduced salivary secretion. Common causes include:

  • Autoimmune diseases: Conditions like Sjögren’s can damage salivary glands and reduce saliva production.
  • Cancer treatments: Radiation therapy to the head and neck can impair salivary glands, leading to dry mouth.
  • Medications: Many medications, including antihistamines, antidepressants, and blood-pressure drugs, list dry mouth as a side effect.

Recognizing these causes supports appropriate assessment and the selection of treatments to manage xerostomia effectively.

What are the symptoms and risks associated with dry mouth?

Symptoms of xerostomia vary but commonly include:

  • Increased thirst: A persistent sensation of oral dryness that increases thirst.
  • Difficulty swallowing: Reduced saliva volume can impair bolus formation and make swallowing food challenging.
  • Dental issues: Reduced salivary cleansing increases the risk of dental caries and periodontal disease.

Untreated xerostomia predisposes people to significant oral problems, highlighting the need for effective relief and prevention.
