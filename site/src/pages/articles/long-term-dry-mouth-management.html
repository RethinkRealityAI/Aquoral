Long-term dry mouth management: a care plan and treatment options

A guide to the causes, symptoms and long-term management of chronic dry mouth, and how to build a care plan that protects your oral health and comfort.

Chronic dry mouth (xerostomia) is a common condition that causes oral discomfort and increases the risk of dental problems. This guide summarises causes, symptoms and evidence-based long-term management. It reviews the main causes, available options and principles for building an individual care plan. Understanding these factors is important because xerostomia can substantially reduce quality of life. The article also reviews how dry mouth develops, the role of products such as Aquoral, and practical measures for symptom control.

What are the primary causes and symptoms of chronic dry mouth?

Xerostomia can result from medications, systemic disease or behavioural factors. Identifying the underlying cause is essential for targeted management. Medications, particularly those with anticholinergic effects, commonly reduce saliva flow. Systemic conditions such as Sjögren’s and cancer therapies may worsen symptoms. Lifestyle or device-related factors, for example CPAP use at night, also reduce oral moisture.

How do medications and medical conditions contribute to xerostomia?

Medication is a frequent cause of xerostomia. Anticholinergic medicines used for depression, allergies, high blood pressure and other disorders decrease saliva. Chronic diseases such as diabetes, and autoimmune conditions such as Sjögren’s, may cause persistent low saliva. Recognising these links helps you and your care team improve symptom control and consider alternatives when appropriate.

Medication-induced xerostomia has a substantial effect on well-being, so managing it is an important part of overall care.

Managing medication-induced dry mouth: causes and treatment. Adverse effects of medications produce xerostomia; in some instances medications have been discontinued owing to severe adverse events. Thus, management of xerostomia is necessary to permit effective treatment of the primary disease. This study evaluated the clinical status of patients with medication-induced xerostomia and examined factors associated with symptomatic improvement.

Characteristics of medication-induced xerostomia and effect of treatment, K Katsura, 2023

What are the key symptoms of long-term dry mouth?

Common features of chronic xerostomia include persistent oral dryness, increased thirst (polydipsia) and difficulty swallowing (dysphagia). People often describe a sticky feeling in the mouth and experience complications such as tooth decay and difficulty speaking. Recognising these signs early allows timely action to protect your oral health.

Which treatment options work best for managing xerostomia long term?

Long-term management usually combines everyday measures with treatments. Topical lubricants and saliva substitutes are the core of symptom relief. Aquoral, an oral spray designed to moisten and lubricate the lining of the mouth, can provide rapid relief. Where appropriate, medicines that stimulate saliva and targeted lifestyle changes may further improve symptoms.

How does Aquoral provide relief?

Aquoral applies moisture directly to the lining of the mouth, giving prompt relief. This lipid-based spray helps hydrate the surfaces of the mouth and may protect against the dental complications associated with chronic dryness. It can be useful for people going through cancer treatment, CPAP users and people with Sjögren’s. Regular use, following the directions on the package, can improve comfort and daily functioning for many people with xerostomia.

In complex disorders such as Sjögren’s, a combination of topical and systemic treatments is often needed to manage persistent dryness.

Sjögren’s dry mouth: topical and systemic treatments. Management of primary Sjögren’s focuses on treating sicca symptoms and systemic disease. Sicca features are addressed symptomatically with topical agents, including saliva substitutes and artificial tears; when residual salivary function exists, sialogogues are the preferred therapy.

Topical and systemic medications for the treatment of primary Sjögren’s, M Ramos-Casals, 2012

What are other saliva substitutes and stimulation methods?

In addition to Aquoral, other saliva substitutes and gland stimulation techniques can support oral hydration. Chewing sugar-free gum increases saliva flow in people who still have some gland function. Staying well hydrated is fundamental. Products made for dry mouth and moisture-rich foods may complement topical treatments and help keep the mouth moist.

Emerging evidence suggests that physical stimulation of the salivary glands may help some people, particularly where gland damage is reversible.

Physical stimulation for dry mouth: systemic causes and treatment outcomes. Treatment response varies by aetiology: patients with Sjögren’s often exhibit limited response, whereas those with medication-induced hyposalivation demonstrate more favourable outcomes irrespective of stimulation strategy. Physical stimulation of salivary glands may therefore be a viable option for cases with reversible glandular dysfunction.

A scoping review on hyposalivation associated with systemic conditions: the role of physical stimulation in the treatment approaches, D Heller, 2023

How can you build a personal dry mouth care plan?

A personal care plan brings relief measures, behaviour changes and monitoring into your daily routine. Keep a record of your symptom patterns and triggers to guide what you try. This structured approach helps improve comfort and protect your oral health over time.

What lifestyle and diet changes support oral hydration?

Lifestyle changes make a real difference. Drink fluids regularly and limit things that dehydrate you, such as caffeine and alcohol. Adding water-rich foods, such as certain fruits and vegetables, can help keep your mouth moist.

How should you monitor symptoms over the long term?

Keeping track of your symptoms is central to long-term care. Regular medication reviews with your doctor or pharmacist can identify medicines that contribute to dry mouth. Learning how to use oral moisturisers and keeping up consistent oral hygiene lets you manage symptoms day to day alongside your regular care.

Where to find guidance and support

Your doctor, dentist and pharmacist can offer evidence-based recommendations and help you decide how products such as Aquoral fit into your routine. Talk to them for an individual assessment and a coordinated plan, and rely on trusted health resources for information.

If you have difficulty swallowing related to dry mouth, your care team can suggest techniques and suitable products to make swallowing easier and improve lubrication. If your dry mouth is linked to a neurological disease, a plan that addresses both the underlying condition and its effects on your mouth works best. Used as directed, Aquoral can be part of a complete strategy to relieve dry mouth and support oral health.
